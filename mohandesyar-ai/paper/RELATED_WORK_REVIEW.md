# Literature positioning for MohandesYar AI 2.0

This reviewer-facing note records the research literature used to position the SoftwareX manuscript. It is not a substitute for the final manuscript text.

## Directly relevant research

1. Nguyen, L. D., Koufakou, A., & Mitchell, C. (2015). *A smart mobile app for site inspection and documentation*. DOI: 10.14288/1.0076358. The InSite Inspector study demonstrates mobile capture of construction-defect images, defect metadata, GPS location, and report generation.
2. Zollmann, S., Hoppe, C., Kluckner, S., Poglitsch, C., Bischof, H., & Reitmayr, G. (2014). *Augmented Reality for Construction Site Monitoring and Documentation*. Proceedings of the IEEE, 102. DOI: 10.1109/JPROC.2013.2294314. This work demonstrates mobile AR for on-site construction monitoring and documentation.
3. Einizinab, S., Khoshelham, K., Winter, S., Christopher, P., Fang, Y., Windholz, E., Radanovic, M., & Hu, S. (2023). *Enabling technologies for remote and virtual inspection of building work*. Automation in Construction, 156, 105096. DOI: 10.1016/j.autcon.2023.105096. This review frames modern remote/virtual inspection in terms of data capture, information extraction, automation, real-time operation, and policy implications.
4. Schall, G., Zollmann, S., & Reitmayr, G. (2013). *Smart Vidente: advances in mobile augmented reality for interactive visualization of underground infrastructure*. Personal and Ubiquitous Computing. DOI: 10.1007/s00779-012-0599-X. This work shows a mobile field-information workflow for inspection, planning, data capture, and as-built surveying.
5. Ballado, K., Trupp, T., & Liu, L. Y. (2003). *Mobile and Sensor-Based Infrastructure Inspections*. DOI: 10.1061/40671(2003)32. This early infrastructure-inspection work motivates the use of mobile computing, sensors, and wireless communication in field inspection.

## Positioning statement

These studies establish that mobile capture, geolocation, reporting, AR, remote inspection, and field information systems are established research directions. MohandesYar AI 2.0 should therefore not claim novelty merely for using a mobile interface, photographs, GPS, or digital reports. Its defensible contribution is the reproducible integration of an offline-first browser workflow, browser-local original media, optional geolocation, per-evidence SHA-256 integrity metadata, portable backup/restore, Persian RTL A4 reporting, and versioned open-software QA without a runtime application server.

## Claims to avoid

- Do not claim that MohandesYar is the first mobile construction-inspection application.
- Do not claim legal chain of custody, trusted timestamps, immutable provenance, or regulatory acceptance.
- Do not claim superiority over QField, ODK Collect, KoboCollect, BIM, GIS, AR, or enterprise field-management systems without comparative data.
- Do not claim improved safety, inspection accuracy, or productivity unless supported by a controlled user study.

## Recommended future empirical study

A paired field study can compare conventional documentation and MohandesYar using task-completion time, report-preparation time, evidence completeness, retrieval time, error rate, offline task-completion rate, and usability/workload measures. Until such data exist, the SoftwareX manuscript should restrict conclusions to verified functionality, reproducibility, and the controlled QA scenario.
